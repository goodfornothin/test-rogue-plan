How to Spot a Great Dance Partner

6 August 2026 · By Bo

Nervous to ask someone to dance? Here is our guide on how to spot someone you know you'll have a good time with.

When you're new to social dancing and don't know many faces on the dance floor, it can be really difficult to take the plunge and ask someone to dance. So here are 5 signs to help you spot someone who you'll have a lovely dance with:

1All their partners are smiling

Sounds obvious, but one of the easiest ways to judge whether someone is a great social dancer isn't by watching them — it's by watching their partner. Are they relaxed? Smiling? Laughing? Do they look comfortable? Great dancers don't "perform" well. They create an enjoyable experience for the person they're dancing with.

2They aren't rushing

Great social dancers don't feel the need to fill every second of the song with moves. They take their time, enjoy the music, and focus on connecting with their partner. Sometimes the person doing less is the person creating the better dance. They're not trying to show off. They're listening, adapting, and making the dance feel like a conversation. The best dancers know that a great dance isn't about how many patterns you can fit into three minutes. It's about how good those three minutes feel.

3They dance with everyone

Watch who they're asking to dance. Do they only dance with the most experienced people in the room, or do they happily dance with beginners too? The best social dancers aren't chasing status — they're there because they genuinely enjoy dancing.

4They're present with their partner

We've all been there. Someone asks you to dance, then spends the whole song looking around the room. Great social dancers make you feel like, for three minutes, you're the only person on the dance floor. They're listening, responding and enjoying the moment, not searching for the next dance.

5They say thank you afterwards

Tiny detail. Huge difference. They don't rush off before the song has even finished. They thank you, smile, and make you feel like they genuinely enjoyed the dance. People remember kindness.

Hope this helps!
