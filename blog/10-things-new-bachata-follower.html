10 Things I Wish I'd Known as a New Bachata Follower

6 August 2026 · By Bo

Ten honest lessons — the ones nobody tells you when you're new, and the ones I had to learn the hard way.

1Dance with everyone

It's tempting to only dance with people you already know or dancers who look "experienced". Don't.

Everyone should be able to dance with anyone no matter their "level". Some of the most enjoyable dances you'll ever have will be with complete beginners. Some advanced dancers will teach you things without saying a word. Every partner offers something different. A new interpretation of the music, a different energy, or simply a reminder that dancing is supposed to be fun.

The more people you dance with, the more you'll grow.

2Focus on how you feel, not how you look

The best dances don't always look the best, but they feel incredible.

We've all been there. You have the most magical dance. You felt completely connected, melting into one another, everything flowing effortlessly. Then someone sends you the video.

You watch the video back to yourself and are horrified. Your arms look awkward, and suddenly the dance you thought was amazing feels a little less impressive.

But social dancing is all about the feeling. Some of the best dances I've ever had look terrible on camera, while some of the dances that look incredible have felt completely disconnected in the moment. It's all about the feeling. And this is something you'll have to learn the hard way.

3You're allowed to dance simply

There is so much pressure to add endless styling or "know every pattern or combo. But at Rogue Bachata, we believe that there should be no fixed patterns.

Be authentic to you. You don't have to match someone else's style. It's easy to spend hours watching Instagram videos and thinking you need to dance exactly like all the Bachata artists. You don't. There's no single "correct" way to express the music.

Simple doesn't mean boring. Every dancer develops their own style over time. Some are playful. Some are chill. Some love body movement, while others prefer clean, simple technique.

Take inspiration from others, but don't feel pressured to copy them. The dancers people remember most aren't the ones who look identical, they're the ones who dance authentically. Your style will evolve naturally as your confidence grows. Connection beats complexity every time.

4Forget the routine. Learn the conversation.

You don't need to know the move before it happens. One of the biggest mindset shifts in bachata is realising you're not trying to remember a routine. As a follow, you don't need to know the move before it happens, you simply need to listen.

Oscar's great tip for following is to "be lazy." It sounds strange at first, but it works. Don't rush to anticipate the next move. Give yourself time to feel the lead before responding. The more patient you are, the more connected the dance becomes. Rogue Lazy has become one of the Rogue Principles.

The best follows aren't predicting what's next. They're responding to what's happening now.

5The best dancers aren't always the ones you notice first

Flashy doesn't always mean good.

Some of the most impressive dancers on the floor aren't the ones doing the biggest moves or attracting the most attention. They're the ones who make their partner feel comfortable, confident, and connected.

A great leader doesn't dance to show off. They adapt to their partner, listen to their movement, and create a dance where both people can enjoy the music together. A great follow does the same. They respond, communicate, and help create that connection.

The best dancers make you feel like an amazing dancer.

6You won't click with every dance partner — and that's normal

Sometimes you'll dance with someone who is objectively an excellent dancer. You might be too. And somehow... the dance just doesn't work. That's okay.

Just like you won't become best friends with every person you meet, you won't have incredible chemistry with every dance partner. Not every great dance is with the most technically skilled dancer. Don't take it personally.

7You're allowed to have boundaries

It's okay to leave a dance midway through if you are feeling uncomfortable. It sounds obvious. But when we are in these situations, it can feel so much harder to leave the dance than it actually is.

We'd like to hope that at dance events we are in a safe environment, but that's not always the case. Someone's moving their hand a bit lower than is comfortable. Your partner giving you weird looks or trying to pull you close when you are resisting. It is so okay to just excuse yourself calmly and leave. Take a breather if you need to.

8You will meet people you would never have crossed paths with otherwise

One of the beautiful things about social dancing is that it brings together people from completely different backgrounds, ages, and careers all connected by music.

You'll spend weeks or even months dancing with someone before finally asking what they do for a living, only to discover they're a magician, an investment banker or a plastic surgeon.

Some of those people will become lifelong friends. Others will become the people you call when you need advice, a recommendation, or simply someone to dance with on a Friday night. You'll build some great connections for when you get yourself into sticky situations.

Dance has a funny way of bringing together people whose paths would probably never have crossed otherwise.

9You will hear bachata music everywhere and you won't be able to resist it

A song comes on in a café and you may even find yourself grabbing someone to dance spontaneously right there and then.

Before long, your Spotify Wrapped has become almost entirely bachata.

Don't fight it. This is your life now.

10Try out other styles!

There are so many different forms of partnered dance. From Tango to West Coast Swing, they all have different things to offer.

You don't have to become an expert in every dance. But trying other styles will almost always make you a better bachata dancer. And you might just discover another dance you fall in love with.

Plus it adds a fresh new layer to your Bachata. It will help you break away from repeating the same patterns, become more adaptable, and develop a deeper understanding of connection. You'll start to recognise different ways of moving, responding, and interpreting music.

Here's a list of dances we recommend trying:

  • Urban Kiz
  • Brazilian Zouk
  • Salsa
  • Tango
  • Forró
  • West Coast Swing
  • Kizomba
  • Hustle

Come as you are — beginners always welcome, no partner needed.
